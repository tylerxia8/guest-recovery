## Executive summary and success

Managers coordinate 51–100 rental properties and 4–10 independent contractor cleaners through Hospitable and SMS. Manual matching, travel planning, acknowledgments, and exception handling create operational risk. The application imports reservations, generates cleaning work, proposes feasible routes, dispatches assignments, records acceptance and completion. Managers retain control of overrides and exceptions; cleaners interact by SMS without requiring an app.

Measure a two-week pre-pilot baseline of manager coordination minutes per day and compare the two-week pilot median, controlling/reporting turnover volume. Target ≥80% reduction. Pilot reliability targets (proposed, require approval): 100% of deadline-bound turnovers ready before the readiness deadline or explicitly escalated before it; zero knowingly infeasible published assignments; 100% assignment deliveries represented with an acknowledgment or unresolved exception. Report on-time completion rate separately from escalation rate; escalation does not count as on-time completion. Proposed on-time completion launch target is ≥99%, with all late events reviewed. Timestamped task, SMS, and exception history provide evidence. Track daily manual intervention count, uncovered work, solver duration, ingestion lag, and delivery failures.

## Domain and lifecycle

A reservation occupies a property from check-in to checkout. Each non-canceled departure produces one cleaning task. A task's release is the source reservation checkout. Its deadline is the earlier of checkout plus 48 elapsed hours and the next active reservation check-in minus the approved readiness buffer. Without a next arrival, checkout plus 48 hours still applies. Manager targets may tighten this limit but cannot extend it. Never infer the deadline from the current date. Completion is a business fact, not an SMS delivery state.

Legacy tasks may retain `pending_deadline` until recomputation; dashboard and scheduler apply the 48-hour ceiling immediately. Tasks: `pending_deadline → ready → assigned → completed`; `canceled` is terminal except explicit manager restoration via audited action. Replanning may return an uncompleted task to ready. Assignments: `planned → published → acknowledged → completed`, with `superseded`, `canceled`, or `blocked` exits before completion. Publication creates immutable schedule versions. SMS delivery states are separate. New assignments do not require or capture compensation. Historical financial snapshots remain unchanged.

## Business rules and proposed defaults

- Business timezone is `America/New_York` for dates, dispatch and deadlines; store instants as UTC `timestamptz`. Daily availability/schedule dates are business-local dates. Interpret DST using the IANA zone, never fixed EST offsets. Reserve UTC instants for ambiguous source times until resolved.
- Request tomorrow’s availability at 9:00 AM Eastern and dispatch the initial assignment emails at 8:00 PM Eastern. Late opt-ins can enter the next feasible schedule update; no response means unavailable. A delayed send uses its actual send time for the 30/60-minute acknowledgment intervals. Configuration changes are versioned and audited.
- Typical checkout/check-in are 10:00 AM/4:00 PM, only used when an authorized source provides date-only values and the manager approves the interpretation; flag inferred timestamps. Actual verified reservation times take precedence. Proposed readiness buffer: 30 minutes.
- Bedroom cleaning defaults (2026-11-11): studio 60 minutes; one bedroom 90; two 120; three 150; add 30 minutes per additional bedroom. A positive house override wins; clearing it restores the bedroom estimate. This replaces the earlier uniform two-hour default. One- and three-bedroom estimates use the upper ends of the cleaner’s reported ranges; the midpoint and other sizes follow a provisional linear pattern. Actual condition, bathrooms and layout can require an override. Completed work retains its recorded duration.
- Next-known-arrival tasks may be placed on any feasible service day after checkout and before deadline; prefer earlier feasible service within a proposed 48-hour pre-arrival planning horizon. This horizon is configurable/manager-approved and never moves a true deadline. If departure is already inside that horizon, plan immediately. No-arrival work is actionable immediately and remains due within 48 elapsed hours of checkout; the planning horizon never postpones that deadline.
- Contractor availability requires an explicit opt-in for each business date in hosted workspaces. Weekly hours define capacity only. Working hours, capacity and default starting location require individual setup; no fabricated universal cleaner hours or capacity. Late availability updates apply after cutoff and trigger replan.
- Acknowledgment applies to one cleaner's current published date/version. Any change to that cleaner's jobs, times, instructions affecting service requires a new version and explicit acknowledgment. Unchanged cleaners retain acceptance.
- Manager locks are hard constraints. A conflicting lock prevents affected publication and raises an exception; it never authorizes an impossible schedule. No automatic reassign of completed work. In-progress status is not a required cleaner command in release 1; assume work may be in progress after planned start and require manager confirmation before automatic reassignment then.
- New reservation activity during outages or stale inputs is a safety concern: block affected publication when source freshness is beyond the proposed 30-minute threshold unless a manager supplies an audited fresh authorized import; an override cannot waive physical feasibility.
- A canceled subsequent arrival changes the predecessor task deadline; it does not cancel the predecessor departure task. Canceling the source departure reservation cancels its unstarted task. Completion after unexpected cancellation requires manager reconciliation rather than deleting history.
- All proposed timing/duration/lead-time settings and policies must be reviewed in [open questions](docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md). Dispatch activation requires recorded policy approval. Engineering defaults are tunable and validated in pilot.

## Scheduling policy

Use OR-Tools Routing/CP-SAT as detailed in [scheduling](docs/SCHEDULING_ENGINE.md). Prioritize feasible cleanings by same-day checkout/check-in turnover first, then earliest actual next check-in, then earliest original checkout. Determine same-day dates in America/New_York; an unknown next check-in follows known arrivals. Preserve coverage of each higher priority group before considering lower groups, total coverage and travel. Within the selected work, favor earlier starts for higher priorities where feasible. Then minimize changes to acknowledged schedules and balance workload. Hard constraints: availability, individual capacities, working hours/breaks, property eligibility, duration, travel, readiness deadlines, non-overlap, locks and completed-work immutability. If full coverage is impossible publish only an independently verified feasible subset with explicit uncovered-work exceptions. Never claim an optimal solution when only a feasible timeout result exists.
